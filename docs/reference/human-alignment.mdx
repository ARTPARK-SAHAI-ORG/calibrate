## Setup

Two annotators, 👩 Asha and 👨 Ben, labelled four replies from a support agent on two dimensions. Each dimension also has a 🤖 evaluator, an LLM judge, that scored the same four replies.

- `Safety (binary)`: each answer is `Pass` or `Fail`.
- `Empathy (rating)`: each answer is a rating from 1 to 5.

| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | <Pass /> | <Pass /> | <Pass /> |
| 2 | <Pass /> | <Fail /> | <Pass /> |
| 3 | <Fail /> | <Fail /> | <Fail /> |
| 4 | <Pass /> | <Pass /> | <Fail /> |

</Tab>
<Tab title="Empathy (rating)">

| --- | --- | --- | --- |

<Note>
Only each person's latest answer is used. If 👩 Asha changes her answer on item 2 from `Fail` to `Pass`, the calculations use `Pass` and ignore the earlier `Fail`.
</Note>

## How is the human score worked out?

For `Safety (binary)`, the human score is the share of labels that are `Pass`. For `Empathy (rating)`, it is the average rating.

| Annotator | Safety (binary) | Empathy (rating) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 👩 Asha | 3 `Pass` of 4 | 4 + 3 + 5 + 2 = 14 |
| 👨 Ben | 2 `Pass` of 4 | 4 + 2 + 5 + 3 = 14 |
| **Human score** | **(3 + 2) of 8 labels = 63%** | **(14 + 14) / 8 labels = 3.5 / 5** |

## How is the evaluator score worked out?

For `Safety (binary)`, the evaluator score is the share of items it scored `Pass`. For `Empathy (rating)`, it is the average rating.

| Item | 🤖 Evaluator on Safety (binary) | 🤖 Evaluator on Empathy (rating) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | <Pass /> | 5 |
| 2 | <Pass /> | 4 |
| 3 | <Fail /> | 4 |
| 4 | <Fail /> | 2 |
| **Evaluator score** | **2 of 4 items = 50%** | **(5 + 4 + 4 + 2) / 4 items = 3.75 / 5** |

<Note>
Only the evaluator's latest score on each item is used. Scores from versions of the evaluator other than the live version are not used.
</Note>

## Do the annotators agree with each other?

Inter-annotator agreement compares 👩 Asha's and 👨 Ben's answers on each item.

<Tabs>
<Tab title="Safety (binary)">

| Item | 👩 Asha | 👨 Ben | Same answer? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | <Pass /> | <Pass /> | <Match /> |
| 2 | <Pass /> | <Fail /> | <NoMatch /> |
| 3 | <Fail /> | <Fail /> | <Match /> |
| 4 | <Pass /> | <Pass /> | <Match /> |
| **Agreement** |  |  | **3 of 4 items = 75%** |

</Tab>
<Tab title="Empathy (rating)">

Two ratings that are close still agree partly:

**Agreement = 1 - difference between the two ratings / (highest rating on the scale - lowest rating on the scale)**

On a scale of 1 to 5, that is 1 - difference / (5 - 1).

| Item | 👩 Asha | 👨 Ben | Agreement |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 4 | 4 | 1 - (4 - 4) / (5 - 1) = 1 |
| 2 | 3 | 2 | 1 - (3 - 2) / (5 - 1) = 0.75 |
| 3 | 5 | 5 | 1 - (5 - 5) / (5 - 1) = 1 |
| 4 | 2 | 3 | 1 - (3 - 2) / (5 - 1) = 0.75 |
| **Agreement** |  |  | **(1 + 0.75 + 1 + 0.75) / 4 items = 88%** |

</Tab>
</Tabs>

## Is a high agreement good enough?

Suppose 👩 Asha and 👨 Ben label 10 other replies on `Safety (binary)`, and each says `Pass` 9 times out of 10 without reading them:

<Answers name="👩 Asha" answers="PPPPPPPPFP" />
<Answers name="👨 Ben" answers="PPPPPPPPPF" />

They agree on 8 of 10 replies, 80%. By chance alone they would agree (0.9 x 0.9) + (0.1 x 0.1) = 82% of the time. So 80% sounds good, but it is slightly worse than chance.

## How much of the agreement is more than luck?

Kappa is agreement after removing what chance alone would produce.

**Kappa = (agreement - chance agreement) / (1 - chance agreement)**

Kappa runs from -1 to 1: 1 is full agreement, 0 is the same as chance, and below 0 is worse than chance.

| Comparison | Agreement | Chance agreement | Kappa |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 👩 Asha and 👨 Ben on `Safety (binary)` | 75% | 53% | (0.75 - 0.53) / (1 - 0.53) = **0.47** |
| 🤖 Evaluator and the annotators on `Safety (binary)` | 63% | 50% | (0.625 - 0.5) / (1 - 0.5) = **0.25** |
| 👩 Asha and 👨 Ben on `Empathy (rating)` | 88% | 69% | (0.875 - 0.6875) / (1 - 0.6875) = **0.6** |
| 🤖 Evaluator and the annotators on `Empathy (rating)` | 75% | 69% | (0.75 - 0.6875) / (1 - 0.6875) = **0.2** |

How each chance agreement is worked out:

- **👩 Asha and 👨 Ben on `Safety (binary)`:** their 8 labels are 5 `Pass` and 3 `Fail`. Chance = (5/8 x 5/8) + (3/8 x 3/8) = 0.53.
- **🤖 Evaluator and the annotators on `Safety (binary)`:** over the 8 pairs, the evaluator says `Pass` 4 times and the annotators 5 times. Chance = (4/8 x 5/8) + (4/8 x 3/8) = 0.5.
- **👩 Asha and 👨 Ben on `Empathy (rating)`:** their 8 ratings are two each of 2, 3, 4 and 5. Over the 16 ways to pick two of these, the agreements add up to (4 x 1) + (6 x 0.75) + (4 x 0.5) + (2 x 0.25) = 11. Chance = 11 / 16 = 0.6875.
- **🤖 Evaluator and the annotators on `Empathy (rating)`:** over the 8 pairs, the evaluator gives 5 twice, 4 four times and 2 twice. The annotators give two each of 2, 3, 4 and 5. Against those, a 5 agrees 0.625 on average, a 4 agrees 0.75 and a 2 agrees 0.625. Chance = (2/8 x 0.625) + (4/8 x 0.75) + (2/8 x 0.625) = 0.6875.

## Why can kappa not be worked out?

| Case | Why | What to do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Every pair comes from one item | One item cannot show what chance alone would produce. | Label more items on this dimension. |
| Everyone gave the same answer | Chance agreement is 100%, so the formula divides by 0. | Nothing is wrong. Kappa can be worked out once someone gives a different answer. |
| Nothing to compare | For example, only one annotator labelled the items. | Have a second annotator label the same items, or run the evaluator. |

## Which kappa is used?

| Comparison | Kappa | Where the chance answers come from |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Annotators with each other | **Fleiss' kappa** | All the annotators' answers in one list, because any annotator could have labelled any item |
| An evaluator with the annotators | **Cohen's kappa** | The evaluator's answers and the annotators' answers kept apart, because each side has its own habits |
| One annotator with the other annotators | **Cohen's kappa** | That annotator's answers and the others' answers kept apart |

For ratings, the kappa is **linear weighted**: close ratings get partial credit in chance agreement too, the same way as in the agreement.

<Note>
`Pass` or `Fail` answers and ratings never go into the same kappa, because chance agreement is worked out differently for each.
</Note>


## Why did a number change?

- **A different evaluator version was made the live one.** Only the live version's scores are used, so the evaluator's agreement and score start again from that version.
- **An annotator was deleted.** Their answers are no longer used, including in evaluation runs that already finished.
- **A labelling job was deleted.** The answers given in that job are no longer used, including in evaluation runs that already finished.
- **An evaluation run was deleted.** Its scores are no longer used.
- **An item was deleted.** It is no longer used, except in evaluation runs that already scored it.
- **An evaluation run is in progress.** Its scores are used as they arrive, before the run finishes.
- **An evaluation run failed.** Its scores are removed, so they are no longer used.
