## How is the human score worked out?

For `Explains step by step (binary)`, the human score is the share of labels that are `Correct`. For `Encouraging tone (rating)`, it is the average rating.

| Annotator | Explains step by step (binary) | Encouraging tone (rating) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 👩 Meena | 3 `Correct` of 4 | 4 + 3 + 5 + 2 = 14 |
| 👨 Kabir | 2 `Correct` of 4 | 4 + 2 + 5 + 3 = 14 |
| **Human score** | **(3 + 2) of 8 labels = 63%** | **(14 + 14) / 8 labels = 3.5 / 5** |

{/* not-a-ui-label: Human score */}

## How is the evaluator score worked out?

For `Explains step by step (binary)`, the evaluator score is the share of items it scored `Correct`. For `Encouraging tone (rating)`, it is the average rating.

| Item | 🤖 Evaluator on Explains step by step (binary) | 🤖 Evaluator on Encouraging tone (rating) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | <Correct /> | 5 |
| 2 | <Correct /> | 4 |
| 3 | <Wrong /> | 4 |
| 4 | <Correct /> | 2 |
| **Evaluator score** | **3 of 4 items = 75%** | **(5 + 4 + 4 + 2) / 4 items = 3.75 / 5** |

{/* not-a-ui-label: 3 of 4 items = 75% */}
{/* not-a-ui-label: Evaluator score */}

<Note>
Only the evaluator's latest score on each item is used. Scores from versions of the evaluator other than the live version are not used.
</Note>

## Do the annotators agree with each other?

Inter-annotator agreement compares 👩 Meena's and 👨 Kabir's answers on each item.

| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | <Correct /> | <Correct /> | <Match /> |
| 2 | <Correct /> | <Wrong /> | <NoMatch /> |
| 3 | <Wrong /> | <Wrong /> | <Match /> |
| 4 | <Correct /> | <Correct /> | <Match /> |

<Tab title="Encouraging tone (rating)">

Two ratings that are close still agree partly:

**Agreement = 1 - difference between the two ratings / (highest rating on the scale - lowest rating on the scale)**

On a scale of 1 to 5, that is 1 - difference / (5 - 1).

| Item | 👩 Meena | 👨 Kabir | Agreement |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 4 | 4 | 1 - (4 - 4) / (5 - 1) = 1 |
| 2 | 3 | 2 | 1 - (3 - 2) / (5 - 1) = 0.75 |
| 3 | 5 | 5 | 1 - (5 - 5) / (5 - 1) = 1 |
| 4 | 2 | 3 | 1 - (3 - 2) / (5 - 1) = 0.75 |
| **Agreement** |  |  | **(1 + 0.75 + 1 + 0.75) / 4 items = 88%** |

</Tab>
</Tabs>

## Is a high agreement good enough?

Suppose 👩 Meena and 👨 Kabir label 10 other replies on `Explains step by step (binary)`, and each says `Correct` 9 times out of 10 without reading them:

<Answers name="👩 Meena" answers="CCCCCCCCWC" />
<Answers name="👨 Kabir" answers="CCCCCCCCCW" />

They agree on 8 of 10 replies, 80%. By chance alone they would agree (0.9 x 0.9) + (0.1 x 0.1) = 82% of the time. So 80% sounds good, but it is slightly worse than chance.

## How much of the agreement is more than luck?

Kappa is agreement after removing what chance alone would produce.

**Kappa = (agreement - chance agreement) / (1 - chance agreement)**

Kappa runs from -1 to 1: 1 is full agreement, 0 is the same as chance, and below 0 is worse than chance.

| Comparison | Agreement | Chance agreement | Kappa |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 👩 Meena and 👨 Kabir on `Explains step by step (binary)` | 75% | 50% | (0.75 - 0.5) / (1 - 0.5) = **0.5** |
| 🤖 Evaluator and the annotators on `Explains step by step (binary)` | 88% | 56% | (0.875 - 0.5625) / (1 - 0.5625) = **0.71** |
| 👩 Meena and 👨 Kabir on `Encouraging tone (rating)` | 88% | 69% | (0.875 - 0.6875) / (1 - 0.6875) = **0.6** |
| 🤖 Evaluator and the annotators on `Encouraging tone (rating)` | 75% | 69% | (0.75 - 0.6875) / (1 - 0.6875) = **0.2** |

How each chance agreement is worked out:

- 👩 Meena and 👨 Kabir on `Explains step by step (binary)`: with exactly two annotators, each person's answers are kept apart ([Cohen's kappa](#which-kappa-is-used)). 👩 Meena says `Correct` 3 times out of 4 and 👨 Kabir 2 times out of 4. Chance = (3/4 x 2/4) + (1/4 x 2/4) = 0.5.
- 🤖 Evaluator and the annotators on `Explains step by step (binary)`: the evaluator and the annotators are kept apart ([Cohen's kappa](#which-kappa-is-used)). Over the 8 pairs, the evaluator says `Correct` 6 times and the annotators 5 times. Chance = (6/8 x 5/8) + (2/8 x 3/8) = 0.5625.
- 👩 Meena and 👨 Kabir on `Encouraging tone (rating)`: kept apart, as above. 👩 Meena's ratings are 2, 3, 4 and 5, and so are 👨 Kabir's. Over the 16 ways to pair one of hers with one of his, the agreements add up to (4 x 1) + (6 x 0.75) + (4 x 0.5) + (2 x 0.25) = 11. Chance = 11 / 16 = 0.6875.
- 🤖 Evaluator and the annotators on `Encouraging tone (rating)`: kept apart, as above. Over the 8 pairs, the evaluator gives 5 twice, 4 four times and 2 twice. The annotators give two each of 2, 3, 4 and 5. Against those, a 5 agrees 0.625 on average, a 4 agrees 0.75 and a 2 agrees 0.625. Chance = (2/8 x 0.625) + (4/8 x 0.75) + (2/8 x 0.625) = 0.6875.

## Why can kappa not be worked out?

| Case | Why | What to do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Every pair comes from one item | One item cannot show what chance alone would produce. | Label more items for this evaluator. |
| Everyone gave the same answer | Chance agreement is 100%, so the formula divides by 0. | Nothing is wrong. Kappa can be worked out once someone gives a different answer. |
| Nothing to compare | For example, only one annotator labelled the items. | Have a second annotator label the same items, or run the evaluator. |

## Which kappa is used?

| Comparison | Kappa | Where the chance answers come from |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Exactly two annotators with each other | **Cohen's kappa** | Each annotator's answers kept apart, because each person has their own habits |
| Three or more annotators with each other | **Fleiss' kappa** | All the annotators' answers in one list, because Cohen's kappa only works for a fixed pair of people |
| An evaluator with the annotators | **Cohen's kappa** | The evaluator's answers and the annotators' answers kept apart, because each side has its own habits |
| One annotator with the other annotators | **Cohen's kappa** | That annotator's answers and the others' answers kept apart |

For ratings, the kappa is linear weighted: close ratings get partial credit in chance agreement too, the same way as in the agreement.

<Note>
`Correct` or `Wrong` answers and ratings never go into the same kappa, because chance agreement is worked out differently for each.
</Note>

<Note>
Only annotators who labelled at least one item that another annotator also labelled are counted. When a third annotator shares an item for an evaluator, its kappa between annotators switches from Cohen's to Fleiss' and can change.
</Note>


## Why did a number change?

- A different evaluator version was made the live one. Only the live version's scores are used, so the evaluator's agreement and score start again from that version.
- An annotator was deleted. Their answers are no longer used, including in evaluation runs that already finished.
- A labelling job was deleted. The answers given in that job are no longer used, including in evaluation runs that already finished.
- An evaluation run was deleted. Its scores are no longer used.
- An item was deleted. It is no longer used, except in evaluation runs that already scored it.
- An evaluation run is in progress. Its scores are used as they arrive, before the run finishes.
- An evaluation run failed. Its scores are removed, so they are no longer used.
