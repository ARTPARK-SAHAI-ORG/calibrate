This page answers three questions:

- What score did the annotators give, and what score did the evaluator give?
- Do the annotators agree with each other?
- Does the evaluator agree with the annotators?

## Setup

Two annotators, Asha and Ben, and one evaluator answered the same four items. The evaluator answers pass or fail.

| Item | Asha | Ben | Evaluator |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | <Pass /> | <Pass /> | <Pass /> |
| 2 | <Pass /> | <Fail /> | <Pass /> |
| 3 | <Fail /> | <Fail /> | <Fail /> |
| 4 | <Pass /> | <Pass /> | <Fail /> |

<Note>
Only the latest answer counts. If Asha changes item 2 from fail to pass, only the pass counts. Comments and reasons are never part of any number.
</Note>

## What score did the annotators and the evaluator give?

A score shows what the answers came to, not how often they match. Scores are on the **Human scores** and **Evaluator scores** cards on a task's **Overview** tab, and in the **Score** column on an evaluation run's page.

- For a binary evaluator, the score is the share of answers that are pass.
- For a rating evaluator, the score is the average rating.

| Score | Answers counted | Pass | Score |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Human score | 8 labels: 4 from Asha, 4 from Ben | 5 | 5 / 8 = **63%** |
| Evaluator score | 4 items | 2 | 2 / 4 = **50%** |

<Note>
The human score counts every annotator's label, with no majority vote. The evaluator score counts its latest score on each item, and on a task only scores from the evaluator's live version count.
</Note>

## How often do they give the same answer?

Agreement is how often two answers match, as a percentage. Every pair of answers on the same item is compared.

| Item | Asha and Ben | Evaluator and Asha | Evaluator and Ben |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | <Match /> | <Match /> | <Match /> |
| 2 | <NoMatch /> | <Match /> | <NoMatch /> |
| 3 | <Match /> | <Match /> | <Match /> |
| 4 | <Match /> | <NoMatch /> | <NoMatch /> |
| **Matches** | **3 of 4** | **3 of 4** | **2 of 4** |

- **Inter-annotator agreement** compares the annotators with each other: 3 of 4 = **75%**.
- **Evaluator agreement with humans** compares the evaluator with each annotator: (3 + 2) of 8 = **63%**.

For ratings, close ratings get partial credit. On a scale of 1 to 5, the widest gap is 5 - 1 = 4:

| Two ratings | Gap | Agreement |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 4 and 4 | 0 | 1 - 0/4 = 100% |
| 4 and 3 | 1 | 1 - 1/4 = 75% |
| 2 and 4 | 2 | 1 - 2/4 = 50% |
| 5 and 1 | 4 | 1 - 4/4 = 0% |

## Is a high agreement good enough?

Kiran and Meera each say pass 9 times out of 10, without looking at the items:

<Answers name="Kiran" answers="PPPPPPPPFP" />
<Answers name="Meera" answers="PPPPPPPPPF" />

They agree on 8 of 10 items, 80%. By chance alone they would agree (0.9 x 0.9) + (0.1 x 0.1) = 82% of the time. So 80% sounds good, but it is slightly worse than chance.

## How much of the agreement is more than luck?

Kappa is agreement after removing what chance alone would produce.

**Kappa = (agreement - chance agreement) / (1 - chance agreement)**

<KappaScale />

| Comparison | Agreement | Chance agreement | Kappa |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Asha and Ben | 75% | 53% | (0.75 - 0.53) / (1 - 0.53) = **0.47** |
| The evaluator and the annotators | 63% | 50% | (0.625 - 0.50) / (1 - 0.50) = **0.25** |
| Kiran and Meera | 80% | 82% | (0.80 - 0.82) / (1 - 0.82) = **-0.11** |

Chance agreement comes from how often each side says pass and fail. Asha's and Ben's 8 labels are 5 pass and 3 fail, so two labels picked at random agree (5/8 x 5/8) + (3/8 x 3/8) = 53% of the time.

## Is the agreement better than chance?

Under each agreement on the **Overview** tab of **Human alignment**, under **Score reliability** on a task, and on an evaluation run's page, there is a bar:

<ChanceBar label="Asha and Ben" agreement={75} chance={53} colour="#16A34A" caption="Better than chance" />
<ChanceBar label="Kiran and Meera" agreement={80} chance={82} colour="#DC2626" caption="Worse than chance" />
<ChanceBar label="Everyone said pass" agreement={100} colour="#9CA3AF" caption="Same as chance, or cannot be compared" />

The bar is filled up to the agreement. The tick marks the agreement expected by chance alone.

## Why is kappa missing?

| Shown in place of kappa | Why | What to do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Too few items** | Every pair compared comes from one item. One item cannot show what chance alone would produce. | Label more items for this evaluator. |
| **Everyone agreed** | Everyone gave the same answer, so chance agreement is 100% and kappa cannot be worked out. | Nothing is wrong. Kappa appears once someone gives a different answer. |
| A dash | There is nothing to compare, for example only one annotator labelled the items. | Have a second annotator label the same items, or run the evaluator. |

<Tip>
Hover over the information icon beside **Too few items** or **Everyone agreed** to see the reason on screen.
</Tip>

## Which kind of kappa is shown?

| Comparison | Kappa | Where the chance answers come from |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Annotators with each other | **Fleiss' kappa** | All the annotators' answers in one list, because any annotator could have labelled any item |
| An evaluator with the annotators | **Cohen's kappa** | The evaluator's answers and the annotators' answers kept apart, because each side has its own habits |
| One annotator with the other annotators | **Cohen's kappa** | That annotator's answers and the others' answers kept apart |

For ratings, the kappa is **linear weighted**: close ratings get partial credit in chance agreement too, the same way as in the agreement beside it.

<Note>
Binary and rating answers never go into the same kappa. This is why the **Annotators** tab has a **Kappa (binary)** and a **Kappa (rating)** column, and why the two cards at the top of the **Overview** tab show agreement only.
</Note>

<Tip>
Hover over the information icon beside a kappa to see which kind it is.
</Tip>

## Why did a number change?

- **Making a different evaluator version the live one.** On a task's **Overview** tab and the **Overview** tab of **Human alignment**, only the live version's scores count. The evaluator's agreement and score start again from that version.
- **Deleting an annotator.** Their answers stop counting everywhere, including on past evaluation runs.
- **Deleting a labelling job.** The answers given in that job stop counting everywhere, including on past evaluation runs.
- **Deleting an evaluation run.** Its scores stop counting.
- **Deleting an item.** It stops counting on the task and on the **Human alignment** pages. Past evaluation run pages keep counting it.
- **An evaluation run in progress.** Its scores count as they arrive, before the run finishes.
- **An evaluation run that fails.** Its scores are removed, so they stop counting.

## Check a number by hand

<AccordionGroup>
<Accordion title="Show the calculation: items with different numbers of annotators">

Each pair of answers counts once, so an item labelled by more people has more pairs and counts for more.

| Item | Asha | Ben | Chitra | Dev | Pairs | Pairs that agree |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Pass | Pass | | | 1 | 1 |
| 2 | Pass | Fail | Pass | | 3 | 1 |
| 3 | Fail | Fail | Fail | Pass | 6 | 3 |

Inter-annotator agreement = (1 + 1 + 3) / (1 + 3 + 6) = 5 / 10 = **50%**. A plain average of the three items would give (1.00 + 0.33 + 0.50) / 3 = 61%, which is not the number Calibrate shows.

Kappa uses the same pairs, so each answer counts once for every pair it is in:

| Item | Annotators | Each answer is in | Answers in the one list |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 2 | 1 pair | 2 pass |
| 2 | 3 | 2 pairs | 4 pass, 2 fail |
| 3 | 4 | 3 pairs | 3 pass, 9 fail |

That gives 20 answers: 9 pass and 11 fail. Chance agreement = (9/20 x 9/20) + (11/20 x 11/20) = 0.505. Kappa = (0.50 - 0.505) / (1 - 0.505) = **-0.01**.

Now add an evaluator that scores the three items pass, fail and fail:

| Item | Evaluator | Annotators | Pairs | Pairs that agree |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Pass | Asha pass, Ben pass | 2 | 2 |
| 2 | Fail | Asha pass, Ben fail, Chitra pass | 3 | 1 |
| 3 | Fail | Asha fail, Ben fail, Chitra fail, Dev pass | 4 | 3 |

Evaluator agreement with humans = (2 + 1 + 3) / (2 + 3 + 4) = 6 / 9 = **67%**. The evaluator's answer on item 3 is compared with four annotators, so it counts four times in chance agreement too. Chance agreement is 0.4691 and kappa is **0.37**.

</Accordion>
<Accordion title="Show the calculation: the two cards at the top">

The **Inter-annotator agreement** and **Evaluator agreement with humans** cards put the pairs from every evaluator into one average. An evaluator compared on more pairs counts for more.

Take the three items in the box above, and add a rating evaluator on a scale of 1 to 5. On item 1, Asha rates it 4 and Ben rates it 3, which agree 0.75. The evaluator scores item 1 as 3, which agrees 0.75 with Asha and 1.00 with Ben.

| Evaluator | Annotator pairs | Sum of annotator agreement | Evaluator pairs | Sum of evaluator agreement |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Binary | 10 | 5 | 9 | 6 |
| Rating | 1 | 0.75 | 2 | 1.75 |

- Inter-annotator agreement on the card = (5 + 0.75) / (10 + 1) = **52%**.
- Evaluator agreement with humans on the card = (6 + 1.75) / (9 + 2) = **70%**.

A plain average of the two evaluators' 67% and 88% would give 77%, which is not the number Calibrate shows.

</Accordion>
<Accordion title="Show the calculation: Fleiss' and Cohen's kappa on the same answers">

Priya says pass on all four items. Ravi says pass, pass, fail, fail. They agree on 2 of 4 items, so agreement is 0.50.

| | Chance agreement | Kappa |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Cohen's kappa** (Priya against the others) | Priya always says pass, Ravi says pass half the time: (1 x 0.5) + (0 x 0.5) = 0.50 | (0.50 - 0.50) / (1 - 0.50) = **0.00** |
| **Fleiss' kappa** (inter-annotator agreement) | One list of 8 answers, 6 pass and 2 fail: (0.75 x 0.75) + (0.25 x 0.25) = 0.625 | (0.50 - 0.625) / (1 - 0.625) = **-0.33** |

Cohen's kappa keeps Priya's habit and Ravi's habit apart, and finds they agree exactly as often as those two habits predict. Fleiss' kappa treats them as one group that says pass on 6 of 8 answers, so chance alone would make them agree 62.5% of the time, more than the 50% they actually agree.

An evaluator against one annotator:

| Item | Evaluator | Asha |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Pass | Pass |
| 2 | Pass | Pass |
| 3 | Fail | Fail |
| 4 | Fail | Pass |

- Agreement = 3 / 4 = 0.75.
- The evaluator says pass on 2 of 4 items. Asha says pass on 3 of 4.
- Chance agreement = (2/4 x 3/4) + (2/4 x 1/4) = 0.50.
- Kappa = (0.75 - 0.50) / (1 - 0.50) = **0.50**.

</Accordion>
<Accordion title="Show the calculation: linear weighted kappa for ratings">

Linear weighting means the credit goes down in equal steps as two ratings move apart. Another common choice, quadratic weighting, gives much more credit to ratings that are close. On a scale of 1 to 5:

| Ratings apart | Linear (used by Calibrate) | Quadratic |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | 1.00 | 1.00 |
| 1 | 0.75 | 0.94 |
| 2 | 0.50 | 0.75 |
| 3 | 0.25 | 0.44 |
| 4 | 0.00 | 0.00 |

Calibrate uses linear weighting because the agreement shown beside kappa uses it. With quadratic weighting, kappa would give 0.94 to two ratings one apart while the agreement beside it gives 0.75.

Two annotators rate four items on a scale of 1 to 5:

| Item | Asha | Ben | Agreement |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 5 | 4 | 0.75 |
| 2 | 4 | 4 | 1.00 |
| 3 | 2 | 3 | 0.75 |
| 4 | 1 | 1 | 1.00 |

- Agreement = (0.75 + 1.00 + 0.75 + 1.00) / 4 = 0.875.
- Chance agreement, worked out over the one list of 8 ratings with the same partial credit, is 0.6094.
- Linear weighted Fleiss' kappa = (0.875 - 0.6094) / (1 - 0.6094) = **0.68**.

</Accordion>
<Accordion title="Show the calculation: ratings outside the scale">

A rating can fall outside the scale, for example when it was given on an older version with a wider scale. When the ratings on an item are further apart than the scale allows, the widest gap is taken from the ratings themselves, so every agreement stays between 0 and 1.

Three annotators rate one item 1, 6 and 7 on a scale of 1 to 5. The ratings span 7 - 1 = 6, which is wider than 4, so the widest gap on this item is 6:

| Two ratings | Agreement |
| --- | --- |
| 1 and 6 | 1 - 5/6 = 0.17 |
| 1 and 7 | 1 - 6/6 = 0.00 |
| 6 and 7 | 1 - 1/6 = 0.83 |

Chance agreement uses the same widest gap as the agreement it is compared with, and two random ratings further apart than that gap get no credit. Add a second item rated 4 and 5, which agree 0.75 on the scale's gap of 4:

- Agreement = (0.17 + 0.00 + 0.83 + 0.75) / 4 = 0.4375.
- Chance agreement is 0.5605, and kappa = (0.4375 - 0.5605) / (1 - 0.5605) = **-0.28**.

When no scale is known for an evaluator, the widest gap on each item is taken from the ratings on that item.

</Accordion>
<Accordion title="Show the calculation: one annotator's agreement and kappa">

An annotator's agreement compares their answer on each item with each other annotator's answer on the same item, across every task in the workspace. It is in the **Agreement with others** column on the **Annotators** tab, and on the annotator's page.

Ben, in the three items from the first box:

| Item | Ben | Other annotators | Pairs | Pairs that agree |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Pass | Asha pass | 1 | 1 |
| 2 | Fail | Asha pass, Chitra pass | 2 | 0 |
| 3 | Fail | Asha fail, Chitra fail, Dev pass | 3 | 2 |

- Ben's agreement = 3 / 6 = **50%**.
- Counted once for each pair, Ben's answers are 1 pass and 5 fail, and the others' are 4 pass and 2 fail.
- Chance agreement = (1/6 x 4/6) + (5/6 x 2/6) = 0.3889.
- **Kappa (binary)** = (0.50 - 0.3889) / (1 - 0.3889) = **0.18**.

Agreement is one number over every evaluator. Kappa is one number for each kind of answer, and an evaluator counts towards it only when its pairs come from at least two items.

- Add a rating evaluator where Ben gave 3 and Asha gave 4 on item 1, which agree 0.75. Ben's agreement = (3 + 0.75) / (6 + 1) = **54%**. **Kappa (rating)** shows **Too few items**, because that evaluator's pairs all come from one item.
- Add a second rating evaluator: on item 1 Ben gives 4 and Asha 5, and on item 2 Ben gives 3 and Asha and Chitra give 2. Each pair agrees 0.75. Ben's agreement = (3 + 0.75 + 2.25) / (6 + 1 + 3) = **60%**. **Kappa (rating)** = **0.31**, from the second rating evaluator only.

</Accordion>
