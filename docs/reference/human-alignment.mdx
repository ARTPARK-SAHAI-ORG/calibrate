## Setup

Two annotators, 👩 Asha and 👨 Ben, labelled four replies from a support agent on two dimensions. Each dimension also has a 🤖 evaluator, an LLM judge, that scored the same four replies.

- `Safety (binary)`: each answer is `Pass` or `Fail`.
- `Empathy (rating)`: each answer is a rating from 1 to 5.

## How is the human score worked out?

For `Safety (binary)`, the human score is the share of labels that are `Pass`. For `Empathy (rating)`, it is the average rating.

| Annotator | Safety (binary) | Empathy (rating) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 👩 Asha | 3 `Pass` of 4 | 4 + 3 + 5 + 2 = 14 |
| 👨 Ben | 2 `Pass` of 4 | 4 + 2 + 5 + 3 = 14 |
| **Human score** | **5 of 8 = 63%** | **28 / 8 = 3.5 / 5** |

## How is the evaluator score worked out?

For `Safety (binary)`, the evaluator score is the share of items it scored `Pass`. For `Empathy (rating)`, it is the average rating.

| Item | 🤖 Safety (binary) | 🤖 Empathy (rating) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | <Pass /> | 5 |
| 2 | <Pass /> | 4 |
| 3 | <Fail /> | 4 |
| 4 | <Fail /> | 2 |
| **Evaluator score** | **2 of 4 = 50%** | **15 / 4 = 3.75 / 5** |

<Note>
Only the evaluator's latest score on each item is used. Scores from versions of the evaluator other than the live version are not used.
</Note>

## Do the annotators agree with each other?

Inter-annotator agreement compares 👩 Asha's and 👨 Ben's answers on each item. For `Empathy (rating)`, ratings that are close still get some agreement: 4 and 4 agree 1, 3 and 2 agree 0.75, 2 and 4 agree 0.5, and 1 and 5 agree 0.

<Accordion title="Show the calculation: items with different numbers of annotators">

Take `Safety (binary)` on items 1 to 3 from the Setup, and add two more annotators, 👧 Chitra and 👦 Dev. Every pair of answers is compared once, so an item labelled by more people has more pairs and more weight.

| Item | 👩 Asha | 👨 Ben | 👧 Chitra | 👦 Dev | Pairs | Pairs that agree |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | `Pass` | `Pass` | | | 1 | 1 |
| 2 | `Pass` | `Fail` | `Pass` | | 3 | 1 |
| 3 | `Fail` | `Fail` | `Fail` | `Pass` | 6 | 3 |

Inter-annotator agreement = (1 + 1 + 3) / (1 + 3 + 6) = 5 / 10 = **50%**. Item 3 has six pairs, so it has six times the weight of item 1.

Kappa uses the same pairs, so each answer is used once for every pair it is in:

| Item | Annotators | Each answer is in | Answers in the one list |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 2 | 1 pair | 2 `Pass` |
| 2 | 3 | 2 pairs | 4 `Pass`, 2 `Fail` |
| 3 | 4 | 3 pairs | 3 `Pass`, 9 `Fail` |

That gives 20 answers: 9 `Pass` and 11 `Fail`. Chance agreement = (9/20 x 9/20) + (11/20 x 11/20) = 0.505. Kappa = (0.5 - 0.505) / (1 - 0.505) = **-0.01**.

</Accordion>
<Accordion title="Show the calculation: ratings outside the scale">

A rating can fall outside the scale, for example when it was given on an older version of the evaluator with a wider scale. When two ratings on an item differ by more than the scale allows, the largest difference is taken from the ratings themselves, so every agreement stays between 0 and 1.

On a scale of 1 to 5, the largest possible difference is 5 - 1 = 4. Suppose 👩 Asha, 👨 Ben and 👧 Chitra rate one item 1, 6 and 7. The largest difference among them is 7 - 1 = 6, so on this item 1 and 7 agree 0:

| Two ratings | Agreement |
| --- | --- |
| 1 and 6 | 1 - 5/6 = 0.17 |
| 1 and 7 | 1 - 6/6 = 0 |
| 6 and 7 | 1 - 1/6 = 0.83 |

Chance agreement uses the same largest difference as the agreement, and two random ratings that differ by more than that get no credit. Add a second item that 👩 Asha and 👨 Ben rate 4 and 5, which agree 0.75 on the normal scale:

- Agreement = (0.17 + 0 + 0.83 + 0.75) / 4 = 0.4375.
- Chance agreement is 0.5605, and kappa = (0.4375 - 0.5605) / (1 - 0.5605) = **-0.28**.

When no scale is known for a dimension, the largest difference on each item is taken from the ratings on that item.

</Accordion>
<Accordion title="Show the calculation: one annotator against the others">

One annotator's agreement compares their answer on each item with each other annotator's answer on the same item, across every task in the workspace.

👨 Ben on `Safety (binary)`, with the four annotators from the box above:

| Item | 👨 Ben | Other annotators | Pairs | Pairs that agree |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | `Pass` | 👩 Asha `Pass` | 1 | 1 |
| 2 | `Fail` | 👩 Asha `Pass`, 👧 Chitra `Pass` | 2 | 0 |
| 3 | `Fail` | 👩 Asha `Fail`, 👧 Chitra `Fail`, 👦 Dev `Pass` | 3 | 2 |

- 👨 Ben's agreement on `Safety (binary)` = 3 / 6 = 50%.
- Taking each answer once for every pair it is in, 👨 Ben's answers are 1 `Pass` and 5 `Fail`, and the others' are 4 `Pass` and 2 `Fail`.
- Chance agreement = (1/6 x 4/6) + (5/6 x 2/6) = 0.3889.
- 👨 Ben's kappa on binary answers = (0.5 - 0.3889) / (1 - 0.3889) = **0.18**.

On `Empathy (rating)`, 👨 Ben is compared with 👩 Asha on the four items in the Setup, which agree 3.5 of 4.

- 👨 Ben's agreement is one number over every dimension: (3 + 3.5) / (6 + 4) = **65%**.
- 👨 Ben's kappa on ratings = (0.875 - 0.6875) / (1 - 0.6875) = **0.6**. With only two annotators, this Cohen's chance agreement comes out the same as the Fleiss' one.

Kappa is one number for each kind of answer, and a dimension is used in it only when its pairs come from at least two items. If 👨 Ben had rated `Empathy (rating)` on item 1 alone, his kappa on ratings could not be worked out, because every pair would come from one item.

</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Show the calculation: agreement across both dimensions">

The overall inter-annotator agreement and the overall evaluator agreement with humans put the pairs from every dimension into one average. A dimension compared on more pairs has more weight.

Take `Safety (binary)` with 👧 Chitra and 👦 Dev added, from the boxes above, and `Empathy (rating)` from the Setup:

| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

| `Empathy (rating)` | 4 | 3.5 | 8 | 6 |

- Overall inter-annotator agreement = (5 + 3.5) / (10 + 4) = **61%**.
- Overall evaluator agreement with humans = (7 + 6) / (9 + 8) = **76%**.

A plain average of the two inter-annotator agreements, 50% and 88%, would give 69%, which is not how the overall agreement is computed.

## Is a high agreement good enough?

Suppose 👩 Asha and 👨 Ben label 10 other replies on `Safety (binary)`, and each says `Pass` 9 times out of 10 without reading them:

<Answers name="👩 Asha" answers="PPPPPPPPFP" />
<Answers name="👨 Ben" answers="PPPPPPPPPF" />

They agree on 8 of 10 replies, 80%. By chance alone they would agree (0.9 x 0.9) + (0.1 x 0.1) = 82% of the time. So 80% sounds good, but it is slightly worse than chance.

## How much of the agreement is more than luck?

Kappa is agreement after removing what chance alone would produce.

**Kappa = (agreement - chance agreement) / (1 - chance agreement)**

<KappaScale />

| Comparison | Agreement | Chance agreement | Kappa |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 👩 Asha and 👨 Ben on `Safety (binary)` | 75% | 53% | (0.75 - 0.53) / (1 - 0.53) = **0.47** |
| 🤖 `Safety (binary)` and the annotators | 63% | 50% | (0.625 - 0.5) / (1 - 0.5) = **0.25** |
| 👩 Asha and 👨 Ben on `Empathy (rating)` | 88% | 69% | (0.875 - 0.6875) / (1 - 0.6875) = **0.6** |
| 🤖 `Empathy (rating)` and the annotators | 75% | 69% | (0.75 - 0.6875) / (1 - 0.6875) = **0.2** |
| 👩 Asha and 👨 Ben, saying `Pass` 9 times in 10 | 80% | 82% | (0.8 - 0.82) / (1 - 0.82) = **-0.11** |

Chance agreement comes from how often each side gives each answer. On `Safety (binary)`, 👩 Asha's and 👨 Ben's 8 labels are 5 `Pass` and 3 `Fail`, so two labels picked at random agree (5/8 x 5/8) + (3/8 x 3/8) = 53% of the time. For `Empathy (rating)`, two random ratings get the same partial credit as in the agreement.

Agreement above chance gives a kappa above 0. Agreement below chance gives a kappa below 0.

## Why can kappa not be worked out?

| Case | Why | What to do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Every pair comes from one item | One item cannot show what chance alone would produce. | Label more items on this dimension. |
| Everyone gave the same answer | Chance agreement is 100%, so the formula divides by 0. | Nothing is wrong. Kappa can be worked out once someone gives a different answer. |
| Nothing to compare | For example, only one annotator labelled the items. | Have a second annotator label the same items, or run the evaluator. |

## Which kappa is used?

| Comparison | Kappa | Where the chance answers come from |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Annotators with each other | **Fleiss' kappa** | All the annotators' answers in one list, because any annotator could have labelled any item |
| An evaluator with the annotators | **Cohen's kappa** | The evaluator's answers and the annotators' answers kept apart, because each side has its own habits |
| One annotator with the other annotators | **Cohen's kappa** | That annotator's answers and the others' answers kept apart |

For ratings, the kappa is **linear weighted**: close ratings get partial credit in chance agreement too, the same way as in the agreement.

<Note>
`Pass` or `Fail` answers and ratings never go into the same kappa, because chance agreement is worked out differently for each.
</Note>

<AccordionGroup>
<Accordion title="Show the calculation: Fleiss' and Cohen's kappa on the same answers">

Suppose 👩 Asha says `Pass` on all four items and 👨 Ben says `Pass`, `Pass`, `Fail`, `Fail`. They agree on 2 of 4 items, so agreement is 0.5.

| | Chance agreement | Kappa |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Cohen's kappa** (👩 Asha against the others) | 👩 Asha always says `Pass`, 👨 Ben says `Pass` half the time: (1 x 0.5) + (0 x 0.5) = 0.5 | (0.5 - 0.5) / (1 - 0.5) = **0** |
| **Fleiss' kappa** (inter-annotator agreement) | One list of 8 answers, 6 `Pass` and 2 `Fail`: (0.75 x 0.75) + (0.25 x 0.25) = 0.625 | (0.5 - 0.625) / (1 - 0.625) = **-0.33** |

Cohen's kappa keeps 👩 Asha's habit and 👨 Ben's habit apart, and finds they agree exactly as often as those two habits predict. Fleiss' kappa treats them as one group that says `Pass` on 6 of 8 answers, so chance alone would make them agree 62.5% of the time, more than the 50% they actually agree.

The 🤖 `Safety (binary)` evaluator against 👩 Asha alone, from the Setup:

| Item | 🤖 Safety (binary) | 👩 Asha |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | `Pass` | `Pass` |
| 2 | `Pass` | `Pass` |
| 3 | `Fail` | `Fail` |
| 4 | `Fail` | `Pass` |

- Agreement = 3 / 4 = 0.75.
- 🤖 `Safety (binary)` says `Pass` on 2 of 4 items. 👩 Asha says `Pass` on 3 of 4.
- Chance agreement = (2/4 x 3/4) + (2/4 x 1/4) = 0.5.
- Cohen's kappa = (0.75 - 0.5) / (1 - 0.5) = **0.5**.

</Accordion>
<Accordion title="Show the calculation: linear weighted kappa for ratings">

Linear weighting means the credit goes down in equal steps as the difference between two ratings grows. Another common choice, quadratic weighting, gives much more credit to ratings that are close. On a scale of 1 to 5:

| Difference between the two ratings | Linear (used here) | Quadratic |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | 1 | 1 |
| 1 | 0.75 | 0.94 |
| 2 | 0.5 | 0.75 |
| 3 | 0.25 | 0.44 |
| 4 | 0 | 0 |

Linear weighting is used because the agreement uses it too. With quadratic weighting, kappa would give 0.94 to two ratings that differ by 1, such as 3 and 4, while the agreement gives 0.75.

On `Empathy (rating)` in the Setup, 👩 Asha and 👨 Ben agree 0.875:

- Their 8 ratings in one list are 2, 2, 3, 3, 4, 4, 5, 5.
- Chance agreement is the average partial credit between two ratings drawn at random from that list: 0.6875.
- Linear weighted Fleiss' kappa = (0.875 - 0.6875) / (1 - 0.6875) = **0.6**.

</Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## Why did a number change?

- **A different evaluator version was made the live one.** Only the live version's scores are used, so the evaluator's agreement and score start again from that version.
- **An annotator was deleted.** Their answers are no longer used, including in evaluation runs that already finished.
- **A labelling job was deleted.** The answers given in that job are no longer used, including in evaluation runs that already finished.
- **An evaluation run was deleted.** Its scores are no longer used.
- **An item was deleted.** It is no longer used, except in evaluation runs that already scored it.
- **An evaluation run is in progress.** Its scores are used as they arrive, before the run finishes.
- **An evaluation run failed.** Its scores are removed, so they are no longer used.
