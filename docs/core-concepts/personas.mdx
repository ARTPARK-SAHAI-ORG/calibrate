A persona describes the student that an AI plays in a [simulation](/app/simulations/overview). It says who the student is and how they talk. It does not say what they want from the conversation. That goes in a [scenario](/core-concepts/scenarios).

## See your personas

In the sidebar, under **Agent simulations**, click **Personas**. The list shows each persona's label, characteristics, gender, language and how often they interrupt.

## Add a persona

Click **Add persona**. A panel opens on the right. To change a persona later, click it in the list.

<Frame>
  <img src="/images/app/simulations-persona-edit.png" alt="The Edit persona panel for Riya, stuck on fractions, with Female, Hindi and Low selected" />
</Frame>

Fill in these fields:

| Field | What to enter |
| --- | --- |
| **Label** | A short name you will recognise, such as "Riya, stuck on fractions" |
| **Characteristics** | Who the student is and how they behave |
| **Gender** | **Male** or **Female**. Voice simulations use a matching voice. |
| **Language** | **English**, **Hindi** or **Kannada**. The language the student speaks. |
| **Interruption sensitivity** | How likely the student is to cut the agent off mid-sentence |

Then click **Add persona** at the bottom of the panel to save it. When you change a persona, the button reads **Save**.

### Example characteristics

The two personas used across these docs:

```text Riya, stuck on fractions
A class 6 student who is stuck on fractions homework. Gets frustrated
quickly and gives short answers.
```

```text Arjun, wants the answer fast
A class 6 student in a hurry who keeps asking for the final answer.
```

Riya speaks Hindi and rarely interrupts. Arjun speaks English and interrupts more often.

### Interruption sensitivity

Interruptions happen only in voice simulations. While the agent is speaking, the student gets a chance to cut in each time the agent says the next part of its reply. The level sets how big that chance is.

| Level | Chance of cutting in each time |
| --- | --- |
| **None** | Never |
| **Low** | 25% |
| **Medium** | 50% |
| **High** | 80% |

## Best practices

1. Be specific. Give the student's age, class and anything else your agent should notice, such as being upset or in a hurry.
2. Say how they talk. Short answers, long answers, polite, impatient.
3. Leave the task out. "Needs help adding 1/2 and 1/4" belongs in a scenario.
4. Make personas that differ. A patient student and an impatient one test your agent in different ways.

### Personas and scenarios

| | Persona | Scenario |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Says | Who the student is and how they behave | What the student wants |
| Example | "A class 6 student in a hurry who keeps asking for the final answer." | "The student asks the tutor to just give the answer to 3/5 + 1/5." |
