Aligning LLM judges with human labels is critical because automated evaluators can drift from human intent over time. Even a high-performing LLM judge may fail to capture the nuances of human judgment in edge cases. The Human Alignment workflow allows you to systematically measure this drift, identify discrepancies, and iteratively refine your evaluator prompts to close the gap between AI and human evaluation.

## How Human Alignment works

The workflow follows an iterative cycle designed to bring your LLM evaluators in sync with human ground truth:

1.  **Collect Human Labels:** Assign items to human annotators to establish a "ground truth" for your data.
2.  **Measure Alignment:** Run your LLM evaluators on the same items and compare their outputs against the human labels.
3.  **Identify Discrepancies:** Use the alignment score and disagreement filters to find where the LLM judge fails to match human intuition.
4.  **Refine & Iterate:** Update your evaluator prompts or criteria based on these findings, and re-run the evaluation to track improvements in alignment.

## Tasks

A labelling task is a named collection of input/output pairs used to collect human labels and run automated evaluations. It serves as the container for your alignment research, where you define the data type and the specific evaluators you want to calibrate.

### Defining your Ground Truth

Tasks allow you to establish a reliable baseline by collecting labels from one or more human annotators. By comparing these labels to your LLM evaluator's scores, Calibrate computes an **Alignment Score** that quantifies how well your AI judge understands your specific requirements.

### Create a Labelling Task

Click **Create new labelling task** to open the configuration dialog.

<Frame>
  <img src="./images/human-alignment-task-create.jpeg" alt="Create labelling task dialog" />
</Frame>

Fill in the following fields:

- **Name** *(required)* — A short identifier for the task, e.g. `Helpfulness review — Q2 batch`.
- **Description** *(optional)* — A one-line summary of what this task is evaluating.
- **Type** *(required, cannot be changed after creation)* — Select the kind of data you will be labelling:

| Type | What it evaluates |
| --- | --- |
| **LLM response** | Given a conversation history, evaluate the agent's next response |
| **Speech to Text** | Evaluate the transcription quality against a reference text |
| **Simulation** | Evaluate the agent's performance across an entire conversation history |

<Note>
  Human labelling for **Text to Speech** evaluators is not supported yet.
</Note>

- **Evaluators** *(at least one required)* — After selecting a type, the evaluator panel appears on the right. Pick one or more evaluators the human annotators will grade against. The available built-in evaluators depend on the type:

<Frame>
  <img src="./images/human-alignment-task-evaluators.jpeg" alt="Evaluator selection panel" />
</Frame>

*LLM response*

| Evaluator | Description |
| --- | --- |
| **Conciseness** | Rates how concise the output is relative to what it needs to convey |
| **Correctness** | Checks whether the assistant's reply matches the user-defined criteria |
| **Faithfulness** | Rates how well the output stays grounded in the supplied context without hallucinating |
| **Helpfulness** | Rates how well the output addresses the user's request |
| **Instruction Following** | Rates how closely the output follows the instructions in the prompt |
| **Safety** | Flags outputs that contain unsafe, harmful, or disallowed content |

*Speech to Text*

| Evaluator | Description |
| --- | --- |
| **Semantic match** | Judges whether the transcription preserves the meaning of the reference text |

*Simulation (Full conversation)*

| Evaluator | Description |
| --- | --- |
| **Empathy & Tone** | Rates how empathetic and appropriate the agent's tone was throughout the conversation |
| **Goal Completion** | Judges whether the agent successfully helped the user achieve their goal |
| **Persona Adherence** | Judges whether the agent stayed consistently in its assigned role throughout the conversation |

Any custom evaluators you have already created automatically appear in this panel alongside the built-in ones — no extra step needed. See [Custom Evaluators](#custom-evaluators) below for how to create them.

Click **Create task** to save. The task opens directly to its **Items** tab so you can start adding data.

## Annotators

Annotators are the human experts who review your data and provide the ground truth labels. Adding multiple annotators to the same task allows you to measure **Inter-Annotator Agreement (IAA)**, which ensures that your human labels are consistent and reliable before you use them to calibrate your AI judges.

### Add an Annotator

Click the **Annotators** tab on the Human alignment page. Type a name in the input field and click **Add**.

<Frame>
  <img src="./images/human-alignment-annotators.jpeg" alt="Annotators tab" />
</Frame>

The annotator list shows each annotator's **Jobs** count and **Current agreement** — the percentage of overlapping items where their labels matched other annotators. Agreement populates once at least two annotators have labelled the same items.

  <img src="./images/human-alignment-label-selected.jpeg" alt="Items selected — Label selected button" />

### Assign Annotators

The **Assign annotators** dialog lists all annotators you have added. Select one or more and click **Assign**. One labelling job is created per selected annotator.

<Frame>
  <img src="./images/human-alignment-assign-annotators.jpeg" alt="Assign annotators dialog" />
</Frame>

The supported combinations are:

| What you select | Annotators assigned | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| All items in a task | 1 annotator | Single full labelling job |
| All items in a task | Multiple annotators | One job per annotator — enables IAA |
| A subset of items | 1 annotator | Partial job covering selected items only |
| A subset of items | Multiple annotators | One job per annotator on those items — enables IAA on the subset |

<Note>
  Assigning the same items to multiple annotators allows Calibrate to calculate
  inter-annotator agreement (IAA) — the percentage of rows where annotators agreed.
  This is a useful quality signal before comparing against your evaluators.
</Note>

If your annotations are already collected outside Calibrate, use **Bulk upload** with the **"Upload existing human labels"** option instead of creating jobs. Follow the **CSV format guidelines** in the upload dialog to ensure your data is formatted correctly.

<Frame>
  <img src="./images/human-alignment-run-in-progress.jpeg" alt="Evaluation run in progress" />

### View Alignment and Disagreements

When the run completes, the **Human agreement** section shows the alignment score for each evaluator — the percentage of items where the evaluator's output matched the human label.

<Frame>
  <img src="./images/human-alignment-results.jpeg" alt="Evaluation run results with alignment scores" />
</Frame>

For each item you can see the evaluator's result alongside every annotator's label side by side, including the evaluator's reasoning. Click **Show disagreements only** to filter to only the items where the evaluator disagreed with the human annotator — these are the cases worth analysing.

<Frame>
  <img src="./images/human-alignment-results-filtered.jpeg" alt="Results filtered to disagreements only" />

A low alignment score is not a failure — it is a signal that your evaluator prompt needs calibration. For example, if the evaluator rates every response the same while humans see meaningful variation, the prompt criteria likely needs to be more specific about what distinguishes each rating level.

### Export Results

Click **Export results** to download the full run as a CSV for offline analysis.

### Iterate on Evaluator Prompts

Use the disagreements to identify patterns — prompt ambiguity, edge cases the evaluator misses, or criteria that need tightening. Then go to **Evaluators**, click the evaluator you want to improve, and click **New version** to create an updated prompt version.

<Frame>
  <img src="./images/human-alignment-evaluator-list.jpeg" alt="Evaluators list" />
</Frame>

The evaluator detail page has two tabs:

**Prompts** — shows every version of the evaluator's judge model and prompt. The version marked **Current** is the live version that runs when you select this evaluator in a run.

<Frame>
  <img src="./images/human-alignment-evaluator-prompts.jpeg" alt="Evaluator prompts tab showing versions" />
</Frame>

**Agreement** — shows the alignment trend chart across versions. Each point on the chart is one evaluation run, plotted against the evaluator version used. As you refine the prompt and re-run, you can see whether agreement with human annotators is improving.

<Frame>
  <img src="./images/human-alignment-evaluator-agreement.jpeg" alt="Evaluator agreement trend chart" />
</Frame>

Go back to your task, run the new version on the same items, and compare the new alignment score. Repeat this cycle — export disagreements, refine the prompt, create a new version, re-run — until the agreement trend reaches a satisfactory level.

## Metrics

### Alignment Score

The Alignment Score measures the percentage of items where the LLM evaluator's output matches the label provided by human annotators. For binary evaluators (Pass/Fail), this is a simple percentage of agreement. For rating evaluators, the score reflects how closely the numeric ratings align. A high alignment score indicates that your evaluator is a reliable proxy for human judgment, while a low score suggests that the evaluator's criteria or judge model needs refinement.

### Inter-Annotator Agreement (IAA)

Inter-Annotator Agreement (IAA) measures the consistency between different human labellers on the same set of items. It is calculated as the percentage of overlapping rows where two or more annotators provided the same label. IAA serves as a vital quality signal: if humans cannot agree on a label, it is unrealistic to expect an LLM judge to align perfectly. High IAA confirms that the labelling task is well-defined and that the human labels provide a reliable ground truth for your evaluators.
